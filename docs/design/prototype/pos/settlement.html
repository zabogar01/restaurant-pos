Purpose

Collect money and close the order exactly. Its own route: entering acquires the server-side checkout lease; the draft it holds is client-side and tab-local until close.

Fixed by the PRD

  • No payment record exists before close. The draft is client-side. FR-G9
  • Exact settlement only. No tolerance, no "close anyway". FR-G5, B-18
  • Revenue is the order total, never the amount tendered. Change is not revenue. FR-G6, B-6, AC-5
  • Cash may exceed the balance; card and custom may not. The two pads are therefore not one component with a different label. FR-G3, G4, B-5
  • Change ceiling is validated before commit and the maximum acceptable cash is shown. FR-M5
  • No tips. Definitively excluded. No tip line, no round-up prompt, no suggested amounts.
  • No gateway and no terminal integration. Every payment is recorded by hand. FR-G1
  • Splitting is by payment method, not by guest. FR-G2
  • Custom payment methods come from back-office settings, not free text here. Ruling I-9, B-24
Wireframe scope

Only Cash and Card switch state in this prototype. Meal voucher and staff account behave exactly as card does — prefilled, capped at the balance — and are drawn inert to keep the state list readable.

Ruling I-13 — the amount is already there

Every tender arrives with the remaining balance already in the field. The ordinary payment — one card, one voucher, the exact cash — is method, Add, Close. Nothing is keyed.

For card and custom that prefilled value is also the maximum, so B-5 is satisfied by construction rather than by a rule the cashier has to remember. The over-balance rejection stays drawn, because a cashier can still key past it and AC-6 requires the refusal to name the maximum.

There is no split mode and no split tab. Keying less than the balance is the split; the remainder stays on the balance and the pad is ready for the next method. A mode the cashier must remember to enter is a mode they forget with a customer waiting, and the balance — not a mode — is what B-18 checks at close.

Cash and card are still two components, not one with a different label FR-G3, G4, B-5. The caption under the field is where that difference is visible instead of merely true.

Ruling I-5

An explicit Cancel payment releases the lease and returns to the order. Releasing a lease you already hold moves no money and needs no approval. A cashier must never wait five minutes because a customer changed their mind.

FR-G9, AC-20

The 90-second actor context expired; the draft survived in this tab. Re-authentication is required before close. Nothing was recorded in between.

FR-G14, FR-J3, AC-30

Takeover requires acknowledging that an external charge may already be in progress, is audited, and rejects the displaced client's close.

FR-G10

A table order cannot close while lines are unsent. A quick sale can — it fires at close (FR-E5). Two different preconditions on one control, which is why the quick-sale variant labels its button differently.

Not visible in a still

  • The lease renews in five-minute steps, expires within five minutes of the client going quiet, and cannot run past fifteen minutes without renewed authentication. FR-G14
  • It survives an application restart with its remaining wall-clock time, not a fresh interval. FR-G14, AC-30
  • A receipt-printer failure never prevents the order closing. FR-G8, B-15
